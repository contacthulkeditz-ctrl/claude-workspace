## PART 0 — What this script is actually doing

Before the shot list, the read. If you generate against the words instead of the mechanism, you get 80 pretty
frames that don't sell anything.

**The script is not about supplements. It's about eight years of being obedient to something that wasn't working,
and the accident that gave her permission to stop.** That's the emotional engine. Every visual choice below serves
one of six jobs:

| Block | Job it does | What the picture must make the viewer feel |
|---|---|---|
| Hook (0:00–0:20) | Pattern-interrupt + full-arc preview | "That's my counter." Recognition, then curiosity about the accident. |
| Problem (0:20–1:15) | Build the graveyard, then make it *cost something* | Accumulation → shame. The husband beat is the real hook, not the bottles. |
| Accident (1:15–2:00) | Re-hook. Remove her agency so she can't be blamed | Relief that she didn't *choose* to quit — it happened to her. |
| Labels (2:00–2:50) | Invalidate the old solution with a specific, checkable mechanism | "Wait, is mine magnesium oxide?" — the viewer gets up and checks. |
| Water + Copper (2:50–3:30) | Move the villain from the pills to the water. New category. | "I never thought about what I'm drinking it down with." |
| Cert (3:30–4:10) | De-risk. Make the claim verifiable, not asserted | Skepticism defused by physical proof they can replicate. |
| Transformation (4:10–5:00) | Pay off in *relational* terms, not symptom terms | The husband line is the close. Not sleep — being fun to be married to again. |
| Close (5:00–5:45) | Micro-commitment, no price, hand off to Page 1 | "I should at least read the breakdown." |

**Three things the visuals must never do:**

1. **Never show the product before 3:30.** The whole first half earns its credibility by being about *her*, not
   about a bottle. If a copper bottle appears at 1:00 it becomes an ad and retention dies.
2. **Never show a smiling person until 4:37.** The one real smile in this film is at the dinner table. If she
   smiles in the hook the arc is dead on arrival.
3. **Never show a price, a discount, a badge, or a "limited time" anything.** Value is communicated by the
   comparison shot at 5:05 (trash bag vs. one bottle) and nothing else.

**The four load-bearing images of the whole film** — if you only nail four, nail these:
`S09` ashwagandha next to her mother's photo · `S14` the empty doorway after "he stopped asking" ·
`S43` the bare counter with 23 ring-marks in the dust · `S57` the magnet sliding off the copper.

## PART 1 — Locks

### 1.1 Character lock (CHAR_REF)

Use the supplied character sheet as `CHAR_REF` on **every single** person-shot. Non-negotiable.

American woman, ~50–60. Ash/silver-blonde shoulder-length hair with a soft natural wave, warm hazel-brown eyes,
fine lines at the eyes and mouth, faint sun damage on the cheekbones, no visible makeup, tortoiseshell reading
glasses. Warm, tired, credible. Suburban everywoman.

**Face rule:** identical face in every frame. If a generation drifts the face, kill it — do not "fix it in the
grade." Wardrobe changes across the timeline; the face does not.

### 1.4 The lighting & grade arc (this is the emotional spine)

Colour does the persuasion. Ride this arc — it should be almost imperceptible shot-to-shot and undeniable across
five minutes.

| Grade | Blocks | Recipe |
|---|---|---|
| **GRADE-A** | 0:00–1:15 | Flat, cool-neutral overcast window light. Desaturated. Greenish-grey shadows. Low contrast, lifted blacks. ~5600K. "Clinical fatigue." |
| **GRADE-B** | 1:15–1:45 | Cold blue pre-dawn. ~4300K. Deep crushed shadows, hard sodium-orange accents from terminal signage. |
| **GRADE-C** | 2:00–2:50 | High-contrast task light — one warm pool on the labels, the rest of the room falling to black. ~3000K key against cool ambient. "Investigation." |
| **GRADE-D** | 2:50–3:30 | Amber archival. Tungsten, sepia-leaning browns, copper glints. Slight halation on highlights. |
| **GRADE-E** | 4:10–4:37 | Warmth returns. ~5000K clean morning daylight, gentle saturation lift, shadows open back up. |
| **GRADE-F** | 4:37–5:45 | Honey-warm practical. ~2900K pendant, rich and soft, creamy rolloff. Resolve. |

Sister's house (1:45–2:00) is the **first warmth in the film** — a small bump toward E inside GRADE-B. That's
deliberate. It's the visual "something changed" before the VO says it.

### 1.5 Camera language

- **Observational documentary.** The camera is a person in the room who isn't announcing themselves.
- **Lenses:** 35mm for rooms, 50mm for her at mid, 85mm for the intimate face holds, 100mm macro for all labels
  and product. Never wider than 28mm — no wide-angle face distortion.
- **Height:** eye-level or slightly below. Overhead only for the counter/trash-bag beats.
- **Movement:** micro-drift handheld, slow push, slow rack. **No drone moves, no orbits, no crash zooms, no
  dutch angles, no lens flares.** Every move should be under 15cm of travel.
- **Depth:** f/2.0 on objects and faces, f/4 on rooms. Let backgrounds go soft — it hides AI artefacts and reads
  as a real lens.

## PART 2 — The flow system (why it won't feel like "scene 1, scene 2, scene 3")

The single biggest thing separating an amateur AI VSL from a winner: **shots don't cut, they hand off.** Every
boundary in the list below is assigned one of six handoffs. Build to the handoff, not to the shot.

| Code | Handoff | How to build it |
|---|---|---|
| **MATCH-SHAPE** | Shot A ends on a shape, Shot B opens on the same shape | Round bottle cap → round clock face. Both at the same size and screen position. |
| **MATCH-ACTION** | A movement starts in A and completes in B | Her hand reaches out of frame right in A; a hand enters frame right in B, same speed. |
| **WHIP-BY** | A foreground object crosses the lens and wipes the frame | Her shoulder, a cabinet door, a passing traveller. Cut at maximum blur. |
| **LIGHT-WIPE** | A shadow or highlight sweeps the frame and you cut inside it | Curtain moves, headlights sweep a wall, she passes a lamp. |
| **PAN-THROUGH** | Camera is moving the same direction on both sides of the cut | A drifts left, B is already drifting left at the same rate. Reads as one continuous move. |
| **FOCUS-HANDOFF** | Rack out to blur at the end of A, open on blur and rack in on B | Best for jumps in time or place. The blur is the transition. |

**Rhythm law:** the cut lands on the *breath*, not on the word. Cut a beat **before** the VO finishes the phrase —
picture leads audio by ~4 frames. That's what makes it feel edited instead of assembled.

**Pacing shape across the film:** 2–3s cuts through the stack and the labels (accumulation, pressure) → 4–6s holds
on the accident and the transformation (let it breathe) → the two longest holds in the whole film are `S14`
(empty doorway) and `S69` (she cries into her pasta). Silence and stillness are the premium moments; you've
earned them by then.
